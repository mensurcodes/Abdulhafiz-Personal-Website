About me

I'm Abdulhafiz, a computer engineering student at Georgia Tech. I like the part of engineering where code becomes something physical: a line of SystemVerilog that turns into gates, a firmware loop that decides how fast a motor spins.

This fall I took a 32-bit RISC-V processor from RTL all the way to a routed chip layout with Silicon Jackets. I wrote the design, built the testbench that caught its bugs, then pushed it through synthesis, place and route, and signoff, cutting total negative slack by 32% along the way. With RoboJackets I write the C++ firmware for an autonomous Micromouse that maps and solves a 16×16 maze.

Before Georgia Tech I studied engineering at Georgia State University, where I built Medlo, a medical record platform, and modeled a four-cylinder engine in Autodesk Inventor. Outside of class I co-founded Egnaw Le Egnaw, a nonprofit that has renovated classrooms and delivered textbooks, computers, and Wi-Fi to under-resourced schools in Ethiopia.

I'm looking for internships in digital design, verification, and embedded systems.

Hardware Aug 2026 – present

RISC-V processor: RTL to physical layout

A 32-bit RV32I processor I took from SystemVerilog to a signed-off layout through Silicon Jackets' digital design, verification, and physical design tracks, in the SkyWater 130 nm process.

  • Design. Built the single-cycle processor in SystemVerilog with SRAM-based instruction and data memory and load-stall control.
  • Verification. Wrote a class-based testbench with a monitor and reference-model scoreboard, plus directed and constrained-random instruction tests. Debugged failures in SimVision waveforms.
  • Physical design. Synthesized to about 7,200 cells, then floorplanned and routed in Cadence Innovus with TCL. Improved total negative slack by 32% with clock gating and high-speed cells.
  • Signoff. Ran DRC and LVS with Cadence Pegasus.

A four-cylinder engine I modeled in Autodesk Inventor at Georgia State University, from single parts to a final assembly with working motion.
